This registry is the machine-checkable Phase 11 source of truth. It imports the
four existing MIR feature-migration entries and all seventeen frozen
MIR-to-Cranelift translator seeds, then adds the two Phase 10-only call
capabilities needed to account for the six connected source cohorts without
pretending that the translator inventory already covers them.

Registry parity means equivalent observable behavior and equivalent failure
classification for this declared inventory. It does not claim full Gust
language parity, identical machine code, identical emitted C, or identical
backend-specific diagnostic prose.

- `source_native_guard`
- `baseline_source`
- `positive_expectation`
- `deferred_expectation`
- `route_owner`
- `migration_status`
- `seed_import`

Allowed route owners are `legacy_exact_shape`, `generic_canonical_mir`, and
`deferred`. Patch 3 moves the six compatibility-baseline entries to
`generic_canonical_mir` while retaining their Phase 10 guards as shadow
evidence. A `none_*` value is an explicit evidence gap or non-applicable field,
never an omitted field. Every deferred entry uses an existing source fixture as
its deferred source lane. Family-level adjacent negative fixtures remain frozen
as `none_inventory_only_until_owner_patch` until their owning migration patch.

## Ownership and evidence rules

- The six non-`none` `baseline_source` values are the complete Phase 10
  compatibility baseline. Their primary route owner is now generic canonical
  MIR; the three Phase 10 source-route guards remain byte-for-byte shadow
  evidence until route retirement.
- The seventeen non-`none` `translator_seed_guard` values must match the
  frozen translator suite exactly.
- The four non-`none` `mir_registry_feature` values must match
  `compiler/MIR_FEATURE_MIGRATION_REGISTRY.md` exactly.
- `canonical_oracle_guard` is always present. Where a dedicated MIR-to-C
